Tool 01 · Click Speed

CPS Test — Clicks Per Second

The CPS Test measures how many times you can click in one second. CPS (clicks per second) decides fights in Minecraft PvP, speeds up building and bridging, and shows up in almost every clicker and FPS warm-up routine. Pick a duration, click the pad as fast as you can, and the counter starts on your first click — no reaction time is wasted.

Short tests (1–5 seconds) measure your burst speed, while 30s and 60s tests measure stamina: most players lose 20–40% of their CPS over a full minute. Your final score includes a rank, and your best score for each duration is saved on this device so you can chase it next visit.

Tips to raise your CPS

  • Keep your wrist planted and click from the finger, not the whole arm — small movements repeat faster.
  • Use a light, short click. Pressing hard adds travel time to every single click.
  • Warm up with a 10s test before gaming; cold fingers are measurably slower.
  • Try the jitter and butterfly techniques in the guide below once regular clicking plateaus.
What is a good CPS score?

For regular clicking, 6–8 CPS is solid, 8–10 is fast, and anything above 10 puts you in pro territory for short tests. Most casual players land between 5 and 7 CPS on a 10-second test.

Does a higher CPS actually help in games?

In Minecraft PvP and clicker games, yes — more clicks means more hits registered and faster bridging. In most FPS titles aim and positioning matter far more, but CPS still helps with semi-auto weapons and building mechanics.

Tool 02 · Reflexes

Reaction Time Test — How Fast Are Your Reflexes?

This test measures the gap between seeing a signal and responding to it — the same reflex that decides peeks in Valorant and CS2, dodges in fighting games, and starts in racing games. Click the pad, wait for it to turn green, then click as fast as you can. Clicking early restarts the round.

You get five rounds. Your score is the average across all five, plus your single best round. The widely cited average human visual reaction time is around 273 ms (a general reference figure) — trained gamers often land between 180 and 230 ms on a good setup. Display and mouse latency add a few milliseconds, so compare your scores on the same device.

Tips for a faster reaction score

  • Stare at the centre of the pad and react to the colour change with your whole visual field — don't try to predict it.
  • Rest your finger lightly on the button so there is no travel distance to cover.
  • Run the test at the same time of day; fatigue can add 30–50 ms.
  • A higher-refresh-rate monitor genuinely helps — the green frame reaches your eyes sooner.
What is the average human reaction time?

Around 273 ms for a visual stimulus is the commonly cited average. Auditory reactions are typically faster (~170 ms) because sound is processed more directly. Your score here also includes your screen and mouse latency.

Can reaction time be trained?

Yes, modestly. Regular practice with tests like this one, FPS aim trainers, and good sleep can shave 20–40 ms off an untrained score. Genetics set the floor, but most players never get close to theirs.

Tips to mash faster

  • Alternate between two fingers (index + middle) — one presses while the other resets.
  • Hit the centre of the spacebar; edges flex and register slower.
  • Keep presses shallow. Bottoming the key out hard wastes time on every hit.
  • Mechanical keyboards with light switches usually register faster than membrane boards.
Why doesn't holding the spacebar down work?

Holding the key triggers your operating system's key-repeat, which is not mashing — this counter ignores key-repeat events and counts only fresh physical presses, so scores stay comparable.

What is a good spacebar score?

In 10 seconds, 50–70 presses is a solid average. Scores above 90 need a deliberate two-finger technique, and 100+ puts you in the top bracket of mashers.

Tips to type faster

  • Accuracy first: a clean 45 WPM beats a sloppy 60, because errors cost double time.
  • Keep your eyes on the text, not the keyboard — touch typing is the single biggest jump.
  • Type in short daily bursts; ten focused minutes beat one long session.
  • Don't backspace every mistake during a test run. Finish the word and keep rhythm.
How is WPM calculated here?

Standard formula: (correct characters ÷ 5) ÷ minutes elapsed. Only characters that match the prompt count, so raw speed with many errors scores lower than clean, steady typing.

What is the average typing speed?

Around 40 WPM for the average computer user. Professional typists sustain 65–75 WPM, and competitive records on short bursts exceed 150 WPM.

Tool 05 · Technique Test

Jitter Click Test — 10 Second Technique Check

Jitter clicking is a technique where you tense and vibrate your forearm so your finger shakes on the mouse button, producing far more clicks than normal clicking — typically 9–13 CPS in trained hands. It became famous in Minecraft PvP, where the extra clicks translate directly into more hits and less knockback taken.

This 10-second test uses the same precise engine as the CPS Test, tuned for technique practice: tense your arm, let your finger vibrate on the button, and see what your jitter is really worth. Compare it against your normal-clicking score above — the gap tells you whether the technique is working or you're just shaking. First time? Read the technique guide first, and take breaks: jitter clicking strains your arm and wears mouse switches faster than normal clicking.

Jitter tips that actually work

  • The vibration comes from the forearm, not the finger — tense the whole arm and let the finger ride it.
  • Aim suffers while jittering. Practise keeping the cursor on a fixed point while you click.
  • Sessions of 10–20 minutes max, with stretches. Pain is a stop signal, not a milestone.
Is jitter clicking allowed in Minecraft?

On most public servers, jitter clicking itself is allowed because it is a physical technique, not a mod or autoclicker. Rules differ per server — Hypixel and others flag inhuman patterns, so check the rules of the server you play on.

Can jitter clicking damage my mouse or hand?

It wears switches faster than normal clicking, and long sessions can strain your forearm and wrist. Keep sessions short, stretch, and stop if you feel pain or numbness. See the safety note in the guide below.

Technique Guide

How to Click Faster: 5 Techniques (Jitter, Butterfly, Drag Clicking)

Regular clicking tops out around 6–7 CPS for most people because one finger can only travel so fast. Every advanced technique below cheats that limit in a different way — by vibrating, alternating fingers, or turning friction into clicks. Learn what each one does before you burn out a mouse practising the wrong one.

1. Jitter Clicking

Tense your forearm and vibrate your whole arm so your finger shakes rapidly on the mouse button, reaching roughly 9–13 CPS. It is the easiest technique to start and the hardest on your arm. Keep sessions short, and practise aiming while jittering — raw CPS means nothing if your cursor wanders off the target.

2. Butterfly Clicking

Alternate two fingers (index and middle) on the same button, one pressing as the other lifts — 10–16 CPS with far less strain than jittering. It needs a mouse with a wide enough button and a low debounce setting so both clicks register. This is the best long-term technique for most players.

3. Drag Clicking

Drag your finger across the button with the right pressure so friction makes the switch bounce and register many clicks — bursts of 15–30+ CPS, famous for god-bridging in Minecraft Bed Wars. It is the hardest to learn, the fastest way to wear out a mouse, and not every mouse can do it: grippy surfaces and certain switches work, smooth office mice don't.

4. Grip and Posture

Whichever technique you use, the base matters: a relaxed claw or fingertip grip, wrist supported, elbow near 90 degrees, and your whole setup at a height where your forearm stays level. Tension you don't need is speed you're not getting — shake your hands out between tests.

5. Mouse Settings and Hardware

If your mouse software offers a debounce-time setting, lowering it (for example to 4 ms) lets fast techniques register fully — and on some servers, settings that produce double-clicks are treated as an unfair advantage, so check the rules first. Light switches, a short click travel, and a clean sensor do more for your score than any "gaming" sticker on the box.

Safety note: Advanced clicking wears mouse switches out faster than normal use, and drag clicking can strip a button's surface over time. The bigger cost can be your hand: stop if you feel pain, tingling or numbness, stretch regularly, and keep technique practice to short sessions. No CPS score is worth a repetitive-strain injury.
